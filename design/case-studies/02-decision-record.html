Problem and context

Brújula was scoped to one institution while the boundaries between curriculum, progress, and simulation were still taking shape. Students declare their own academic data; it is not an official record. Privacy limits what staff can see.

Decision records

Decision summaries To confirm

01

Where should the deployment boundary be?

Not chosen Microservices
Separate services would add distributed-system overhead before module boundaries were settled.
Chosen Modular monolith
Spring Modulith keeps curriculum, progress, and simulation in one Spring Boot process.

Consequence: Avoids a distributed system at the start, but modules share a JVM and a deployment lifecycle.

“single Spring Boot process”

02

How should the API contract stay in sync?

Not chosen Hand-written contract
Code kept by hand can drift from the contract.
Not chosen Validation only
Checks the contract but does not generate the client.
Chosen Generate server and client
Use the backend OpenAPI contract as the source for generation, including the frontend’s Axios client.

Consequence: Generation reduces the risk of drift, but the contract copy must stay in sync and the client must be regenerated.

“authoritative”

03

What student grades can staff see?

Not chosen Individual grade access
Would expose grades attached to individual students.
Chosen Aggregates only
The MVP limits staff access to aggregate data.

Consequence: Helping an individual student would require a separate delegated-access feature.

“no access to individual student grades”
